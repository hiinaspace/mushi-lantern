# M4 visual pass — approved sequence and first headset slice, 2026-09-24

This approved sequence reconciles the September 24 inbox visual list with the implemented M1 night grove, M2 staff, M3 audio, and the Sunday September 27, 18:00 Denver jam deadline. It does not replace the accepted GPU shoal behavior. The current project tracker and original charter still describe an upward sky migration; the user's newer direction supersedes that image: the migration is **below the ground, visible through it when adapted**, while a separate Milky Way remains overhead. Returned mushi descend into the migration. The attached Mushishi frames are composition references, not assets to copy into the game.

## Scene contract

- In ordinary clear light the ground and grove remain readable and the migration is absent or barely perceptible. Shade or park the lantern and wait: nearby mushi, bioluminescent foliage, then a broad underground flow appear as night vision rises. Keep the sky's existing star/Milky Way reveal, and align the two bands into a recognizable parallel composition at one authored tutorial viewpoint.
- The underground river runs across the playable grove with apparent depth below the terrain, directional movement, varied density, and a local bend, pinch or loop under the goal. It can be a separate bounded GPU particle or instanced visual system. The 1024 gameplay mushi retain their GPU simulation, identity, one-time score and loose trio behavior. Their existing committed visual phase bends downward and crosses the terrain into the decorative flow; the flow does not need a one-to-one particle count.
- A dim shrine/gate and small contained ember fires replace the 300 m green beam as the proposed goal landmark. The primary lost-player orientation action is to shade or extinguish the lantern, wait to adapt, and find the flow's bend under the goal. Keep the beam as a comparison toggle through the headset review. Ember glare should not erase the reveal; the current adaptation model responds to the lantern, so any ember effect on adaptation is a separate design choice. The shrine's first progress cue shows total returned only; no directional sectors in this pass.
- In full clear light, distant gameplay mushi fade gently after stars fade; nearby ones stay trackable. They recover visibility faster when the shutter closes or the filter switches to blue/orange, and remain fully visible while herding with those colors. This visibility curve changes neither GPU behavior nor return accounting.
- The screenshot's high-contrast band and canopy framing guide the silhouette and reveal. Match neither the anime's individual frame nor its colors literally; preserve this game's colored, legible mushi and dark forest.
