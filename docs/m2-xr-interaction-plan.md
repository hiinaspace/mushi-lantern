## Goal and confirmed choices

- A staff held at different points along its shaft carries a lantern on a damped hanging joint. Release briefly floats the staff, then it settles upright on nearby safe ground with its shutter and filter unchanged, and remains until grabbed or recalled. Its square housing emits primarily forward, with filter glyphs and a shutter line on multiple faces; front slats and a spotlight cookie show horizontal light strips at partial aperture while mechanics retain one overall aperture value.
- Either controller hand can hold or operate the lantern. While holding the staff, bring the free hand near the lantern and press grip to engage a generous snap zone. Raise/lower the adjusting hand to open/close the shutter; yaw the controller left / center / right for blue / clear / orange. The level control frame follows the lantern, allowing locomotion while adjusting. The one-controller route parks the staff, adjusts the lantern with that hand, then regrabs the shaft.
- Default two-controller locomotion uses left stick Y for forward/back, left stick X for strafe, and right stick X for smooth turning; snap turning remains an option. The one-controller variant uses its stick Y for forward/back and X for turning, without strafe. Prim has this either-hand mapping in `/home/s/code/prim/project/main.gd:532-535`; implementing this fallback can follow the initial two-controller VR slice, but it is required for the final game. Beyond/Monado on sayu is the first headset/runtime check.
- Y/B on either controller opens a laser-pointer 2D menu. It may initially host the existing F1 controls/HUD or a placeholder; later it should hold settings, join/host and quit. Holding X/A on either controller for about 500 ms recalls the staff toward that hand, floating through geometry to a nearby hover point until grip takes it or the button is released. Releasing X/A chooses safe ground near the staff's current position, even if recall stops early. This is an intentional recall action, distinct from grip.
- Desktop keeps WASD and mouselook, adds scroll shutter, 1/2/3 filters with a short mechanical/light transition, and holds left mouse to aim/wave the lantern in a bounded screen-space region before returning to mouselook. Desktop should also be able to park/recover the staff.
- First-person VRM/IK, realistic EZ-Tree or scanned grove assets, and final adaptation tweaks are desired later. They are separate from the first tracked-tool gate; a useful, readable staff/lantern mesh and floating hands suffice here.
- The user wants XR Tools adopted as the main XR interaction/locomotion base if practical, including its desktop mode and Pickable/center-screen pickup behavior. Several fixed shaft grab/snap points may approximate free sliding grip. Vendoring and project-specific patches are acceptable; strict upstream compatibility is not a constraint.

## Proposed sequence and open decisions

1. **Get a minimal stereo scene running first.** Add OpenXR startup and a small XR origin/camera/left-right controllers, with an explicit desktop launch path selected at startup. Inspect and pin a compatible XR Tools release; favor its player/locomotion, hand and Pickable components, including desktop support, and patch it locally where this game's interaction requires. Add controller tracking/loss and recenter handling, basic hands and two-controller smooth locomotion against the terrain collider. Use head-relative horizontal movement, turn about the tracked head position, room-scale body following, and seated-height calibration; headset speed must be tuned separately from the current 5.4 m/s desktop speed. Keep the staff inert for this first visual gate. Bring the existing grove into stereo immediately and inspect custom sky, glyphs, foliage, shadows and bloom in both eyes. Establish a minimal Windows OpenXR export and test route early, before detailed staff work. Keep the custom lamp gesture and parking controller outside generic grab logic where needed; avoid two systems writing the same pose.
2. **One shared tool state, two input drivers.** Move staff/lantern under a world-space tool node instead of under the desktop camera. Desktop and XR send poses and commands through the same boundary. One owner writes the staff transform at a time: XR Tools pickup while grabbed, parking controller while unheld, desktop driver while desktop-held. The same effective emitter pose and aperture drive SpotLight and `LightField`; local player adaptation consumes the effective light state plus the viewer relationship to the lamp. Preserve fixed-step GPU input and avoid player-body impulses. **Open decision:** clean CPU/ground/UI branches before or after extracting this boundary. Lean: remove branches directly entangled with player/tool construction now, defer broad test deletion until a working XR slice. Rejected: an unrelated wholesale refactor immediately before stereo bring-up.
3. **Handling state machine.** Placement states `HELD`, `FLOATING`, `SETTLING`, `PARKED`, `RECALL_HOVER`; adjustment ownership is separate. Prototype identifiable grip/snap points along the shaft, with either-hand poses, and assess whether the placement choices feel continuous enough; a projected shaft grip chosen once per grab is the bounded fallback. Use a world-space hanging bob with gravity and drag, and rebase on tracking jumps. Intentional *final shaft release* preserves the shutter and begins float then ground parking; adjustment release, hand transfer and tracking loss do not. Park by finding a nearby reachable terrain point of acceptable slope, obstacle clearance and basin membership; if no candidate exists, remain visibly floating and recallable. Preserve the last horizontal beam aim and let the lantern hang level at rest. After a 500 ms X/A hold, move the same staff toward a hover point by that controller, ignoring geometry; it is grabbable until grip or X/A release. Define the release outcome explicitly during implementation so the staff remains recoverable. **Open decision:** whether the pinned XR Tools Pickable release supports a clean frozen release and atomic handoff. Current upstream source does, but its `SecondHandGrab.SWAP` calls `let_go()` and emits `dropped` during transfer, so `dropped` cannot itself mean final gameplay release. Set frozen release, zero unwanted velocity, and reconcile ownership before parking. Rejected: letting ordinary rigid-body release decide where the gameplay lamp ends up. [Current source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GodotVR/godot-xr-tools/master/addons/godot-xr-tools/objects/pickable.gd)
4. **Filter/shutter gesture.** Resolve a grip press once, in order: current interaction owner, nearby lantern control zone, then nearby shaft. Highlight the target before pressing; require release-to-rearm so adjustment release cannot become a new shaft grab. During adjustment capture controller position/orientation and current control values in a level, lantern-relative frame; relative vertical hand displacement maps to continuous aperture, and horizontal controller yaw snaps to three filter detents with hysteresis. Freeze or strongly damp swing while adjusting. One controller can adjust a parked lamp; keep stick locomotion active while adjusting. Button/menu equivalents remain available for limited reach or tracking loss. Mechanical filter change uses a short close/change/reopen action with one effective aperture and discrete mode shared by lighting, simulation and adaptation. **Open decision:** exact sensitivity/detents after the revised headset test. Rejected: tiny physical knobs requiring millimeter aiming.
5. **Desktop analogue.** Keep existing movement/mouselook and filter keys. Scroll adjusts shutter; left mouse hold redirects relative motion into bounded lamp/staff aiming, with visible swing and a gentle return. Give drop, recall and regrab explicit keys or mouse actions and show concise control hints. Evaluate XR Tools' desktop pickup path against the current first-person controls before duplicating it. The desktop driver uses the same tool state; its camera stays independent. Scroll is active while mouse captured unless a UI panel is open.
6. **Human gate and follow-on.** Test leading a small group around a trunk, changing controls two-handed, parking/recalling, seated reach, tracking loss, hand transfer, cliff descent and recovery. Add and test the either-hand one-controller fallback before the final game and friend playtest. Y/B opens a laser-pointer 2D menu from either hand; first content can be placeholder/debug, then expand to settings and later multiplayer/quit. Measure delivered frame times at headset refresh with 1024 and 512 agents. Give player adaptation a simple smooth distance falloff from a parked lamp so leaving it behind restores night vision; defer exact exposure modeling and other players' lantern influence until multiplayer is actually designed. After the supported export route and one short herding/delivery session work, refine eye adaptation and replace representative grove meshes with provenance and culling/LOD intact. VRM/IK stays after interaction is known to work. The jam still needs a real Windows OpenXR runtime check and first-time-player loop, beyond a sayu Linux headset check.

## Scope limits and implementation handoff

This plan does not reopen GPU flock tuning, multiplayer, full-body IK, authored guide/NPC, or a broad physics inventory. The first implementation handoff is minimal XR startup and stereo grove inspection on Beyond/Monado, with an early Windows OpenXR export route. Subsequent Sol/Luna implementation tasks should have non-overlapping file ownership and be checked against the explicit pickup/parking contract above. Headset interaction, readability and timing remain human and target-device gates.
